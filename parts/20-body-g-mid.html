08

What happens after you own it

The purchase is the short part. Holding the land and building on it is where the ongoing money goes, and both are governed by rules worth knowing before you choose a lot rather than after.

Holding an empty lot

Real property tax in a province is capped at one percent of assessed value under the Local Government Code, plus a flat one percent Special Education Fund. Assessed value is not market value. For residential land the assessment level is capped at twenty percent of fair market value, so at the statutory ceiling the annual bill lands near four tenths of one percent of the assessor's valuation.

Paying early earns a discount of up to twenty percent. Paying late costs two percent per month, capped at thirty six months, so a forgotten lot can accumulate seventy two percent in penalties.

Then there is the idle land tax, and it turns on one detail. A standalone non-agricultural lot has to exceed 1,000 square metres to count as idle, so a lot inside your band escapes it. But the same section applies regardless of area to residential lots in a duly approved subdivision that have been transferred to individual owners. If you buy developer-subdivided inventory and leave it empty, the levy can reach five percent of assessed value and roughly triple your holding cost.

Whether Palawan actually imposes it is a separate question. The provision is permissive, so it only exists locally if the province enacted it by ordinance. Ask the Municipal Assessor and the Treasurer directly, because the provincial revenue code is not published anywhere online.

Permits, in the order you need them

  1. Barangay clearance, from the barangay the lot sits in.
  2. ECAN zoning certification from PCSD, ₱200. Do this one before you even buy.
  3. SEP clearance from PCSD. DENR cannot issue anything without it, and neither can the municipality.
  4. Locational and zoning clearance from the Municipal Planning Office. Ask them here, in writing, how much of the lot is buildable once the coastal easement is taken off.
  5. Building permit from the Office of the Building Official under the National Building Code.
  6. Certificate of occupancy after final inspection, which the Code puts at thirty days.

A relocation survey by a licensed geodetic engineer is not optional in San Vicente. The municipality's own citizen's charter lists it as a checklist item for a zoning clearance, and an approved survey plan is required for a new tax declaration on untitled land.

Design rules inside the tourism zone

If your lot is on Long Beach, the master plan also sets a height limit described as nothing above the coconut trees, a fifty percent cap on lot occupancy, and adaptive architecture with no livable space on the ground floor. The airport's aerodrome clearances constrain height as well. So no multi-storey view tower, and the house you are picturing may need redrawing before it is legal.

    Something you should know about the local agencies

    Four websites that present themselves as separate San Vicente agencies share one hosting block, one office address at the San Vicente public market, and one brand story. They are one operator with four domains. None of them publishes a PRC licence number or a single priced listing, while all of them claim to be licensed. The operators physically closest to your target coast are also the least transparent about who they are. That does not make them dishonest, but it does mean you ask for the licence number first.

    Only a PRC-licensed broker may legally act as one under the Real Estate Service Act. Not one licence number in this brief has been checked against the PRC register. Ask for the number, then check it yourself.
