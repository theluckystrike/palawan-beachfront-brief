How people lose money here

Not scare stories. Mechanisms, each with the check that defeats it, drawn from statute and from decided Supreme Court cases. Read the walk-away list even if you read nothing else on this page.

Walk away, no discussion

Any one of these ends it. Not renegotiates it, ends it. There will be another lot.

  • The seller's copy of the title does not match the Registry of Deeds copy on number, owner, area or technical description.
  • The seller refuses, delays or puts conditions on you getting that copy yourself.
  • DENR cannot certify the land as Alienable and Disposable, or certifies it as timberland, forest, mangrove or unclassified public forest.
  • The survey shows the saleable area falls wholly or largely inside the foreshore or the public shore easement.
  • The land is sold as private but is actually foreshore. Foreshore can be leased and never sold.
  • The technical description overlaps a neighbouring registered title, or the survey monuments cannot be found on the ground.
  • An estate is unsettled and there is no registered extrajudicial settlement, no proof of publication, no estate tax clearance, or an heir nobody can account for.
  • The deal runs on a power of attorney you cannot verify with the principal and with the notary's register.
  • An adverse claim, a notice of lis pendens, a levy or a pending case is annotated on the title.
  • The land overlaps a certified ancestral domain with no NCIP certification.
  • PCSD puts the lot in a core zone, or in a zone where what you intend cannot be cleared.
  • The seller insists on an under-declared price on the deed as a condition of the sale.
  • The structure offered to you needs a foreign buyer to hold land through a Filipino nominee.
  • The seller wants the full price, or most of it, before the transfer documents and the tax clearance exist.

The sixteen tells

Softer than the list on the left. Each one means stop and check something specific, not necessarily stop entirely.

  • Only a tax declaration, and the title is described as being processed or as part of a mother title.
  • The seller wants to supply his own certified copy rather than let you pull one.
  • Any variance at all between his copy and the registry's copy.
  • You checked the title only through the free online service and called that due diligence.
  • The title is a reconstituted one, issued after the original was lost or destroyed.
  • The lot is beachfront and the fence or the technical description runs to the water.
  • Nobody can produce a DENR certification naming the classification order and the land classification project map number.
  • The parcel sits in or near mangrove, nipa or swampy ground.
  • No ECAN zoning certification, and nobody mentions an SEP clearance.
  • The owner has died, or the sellers are the heirs, or one heir is abroad and will sign later.
  • You are dealing through a power of attorney rather than the registered owner.
  • The broker cannot show a current PRC licence.
  • Access runs on a neighbour's goodwill, a dirt track, or a verbal you can always pass here.
  • The seller asks you to write a lower price on the deed.
  • Pressure to pay a reservation fee today, before any document has been checked.
  • You are foreign and are told to just put it in your partner's name.

The mechanisms, and what beats each one

The free online title check is not due diligence, and the LRA says so

This one catches careful people, which is what makes it worth putting first. The Land Registration Authority runs an online title verification service, and its own circular states that the results are for information purposes only, are not a substitute for officially issued copies, and do not serve as a basis for due diligence activities.

The only real check is a certified true copy pulled from the Registry of Deeds, compared line by line against the seller's owner's duplicate. Number, registered owner, area, technical description, and every annotation on the back.

A quiet enforcement mechanism sits behind that. Under a 2023 LRA circular, a patently fake certificate of title presented at a Registry of Deeds is confiscated on the spot and referred to the LRA's investigation division within five days. The registry is where fakes go to die.

Timberland sold as land you can title

Somebody has a tax declaration, has farmed the land for thirty years, and sells in good faith. The land is forest or unclassified public forest and can never be titled by anyone. The buyer discovers this after paying.

Courts have held that swampy ground covered by mangrove and nipa may be classified as forest land, and that possession of forest land confers nothing however long it runs. So an unbroken family history is not evidence of anything.

What beats it. A DENR certification that names the specific classification order or proclamation and the land classification project map number. RA 11573 requires exactly that content, signed by a designated DENR geodetic engineer, which turns a vague and reassuring DENR letter into a checkable red flag. If the paper does not name the order and the map number, it is not the document you asked for.

Double sale of tax declaration land

Unregistered land has no central register, so there is no public record of a prior sale and nothing gives you notice of one. The same parcel gets sold twice, sometimes years apart, sometimes to relatives of the same family. Article 1544 of the Civil Code governs who wins, and the answer often turns on registration and good faith rather than on who paid first.

What beats it. Prefer titled land. Where you cannot, ask at the barangay, walk the boundary with the neighbours present, and check whether anyone else is paying tax on it. Then price the risk into the offer instead of pretending it is not there.

The beach that was never the seller's to sell

Shores are property of public dominion under the Civil Code. Foreshore may be disposed of by lease only and not otherwise. Yet lots are routinely marketed with the beach included, fences run to the water, and technical descriptions get drawn optimistically.

What beats it. A relocation survey by a licensed geodetic engineer, plotting the boundary against the high water mark. If a substantial part of what you are buying sits seaward of the easement line, you are paying private prices for public land, and no title will fix that.

The heirs who were not all there

An owner dies, some of the family sells, and one heir was abroad and never signed. Years later that heir appears. Philippine procedure keeps a two year window after an extrajudicial settlement during which an omitted heir can come forward, and a forged power of attorney or a forged deed can produce a registered title that later unravels.

What beats it. The registered extrajudicial settlement, proof it was published in a newspaper, the estate tax clearance, and every single heir signing or giving a verified power of attorney. Meet the principal, on video at minimum, and check the power of attorney against the notary's register at the Clerk of Court.

The nominee arrangement, for a foreign buyer

Of everything on this page, this one ends worst, and it is not a grey area. The Supreme Court has refused foreign buyers even the return of their own money in these arrangements, treating both sides as equally at fault so that no relief is available to either. Muller and Beumer are the cases. The money is simply gone.

The Anti-Dummy Law then adds criminal liability for both the Filipino who lends their name and the foreigner who profits, plus forfeiture of the property. The sources consulted here disagree on the exact term of imprisonment, one reading the original text as two to ten years and another as five to fifteen after amendment. The disagreement does not matter much. It is prison and you lose the land.

What beats it. Answer the citizenship question honestly and pick a structure that is actually legal for you. If that is a twenty five year lease, take the lease and write the renewal terms carefully, because renewal is by agreement and not by right.

The unlicensed agent

The Real Estate Service Act bars anyone from practising real estate service without a PRC licence, and doubles the penalty for unlicensed practice, with a fine starting at ₱100,000 or imprisonment starting at two years. An unlicensed agent has no accountability, no professional insurance and nothing to lose.

What beats it. Ask for the licence number in the first message, then check it. In this market, four websites presenting as separate San Vicente agencies turned out to be one operator, and not one of them publishes a licence number.

The under-declared deed

A seller asks you to write a lower price on the deed so his capital gains tax falls. It is presented as a favour and a saving you share. It is neither.

Tax is charged on the higher of the selling price, the BIR zonal value and the assessor's value, so the declared price does not lower the floor. Meanwhile your acquisition cost on paper is now lower, which means a bigger capital gains bill when you sell, and you have signed a document misstating a material fact to a tax authority.

What beats it. Refuse in writing, and keep the message. It costs you nothing and it changes how the rest of the negotiation is conducted.

One myth corrected

Buyers get told that a fraudulent land sale is syndicated estafa with heavy penalties, as if the criminal law were a safety net. It usually is not. Syndicated estafa needs five or more people acting together and funds solicited from the public or from members of an association. A single fraudulent land sale is ordinary estafa.

Which is the whole point of this section. The criminal remedy is slow, uncertain and a poor substitute for a ₱200 zoning certification and a certified copy of a title obtained before you pay.

How to structure the money

A contract to sell is a promise to transfer once conditions are met. A deed of absolute sale transfers now, and it must be notarised before it can be registered. Stage every payment against a document that exists, never against a promise that one will. Pay nothing before you hold the certified copy of the title, the land classification certification and the relocation survey. If a seller demands a reservation, keep it small and make it expressly refundable in writing.
